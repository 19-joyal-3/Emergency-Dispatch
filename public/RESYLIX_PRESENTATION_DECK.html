Slide 02 • Problem Statement

The Disaster Telemetry Dilemma

Why Standard Consumer Navigation & Cloud Apps Fail in Catastrophes

⚡ Infrastructure Blackouts

Severe storms snap aerial optical fiber cables and flood distribution transformers. Cell towers lose backup battery within 45 minutes, creating total cellular dead-zones.

🌧️ Dynamic Road Severance

Flash floods, mudslides, and collapsed ghat bridges (e.g. Wayanad Chooralmala / Mundakkai) instantly make predefined highway routes hazardous death-traps without warning.

📱 Cloud-Only App Crashes

Modern smartphone apps rely on continuous HTTP handshakes. When connection drops, map tiles fail to render, routing returns timeouts, and situational awareness drops to 0%.

Slide 04 • Geospatial Mapping

Tactical Map Engine & Offline Vector Basemaps

Multi-Spectrum Cartography with Zero-Bandwidth PMTiles Vector Archives

  • A
    Multi-Source Layers: Esri World Dark (tactical night), Copernicus Sentinel-2 Satellite, and Humanitarian OpenStreetMap (relief roads).
  • B
    Offline Vector Basemap: Pure vector math renders Kerala borders, 5 lifeline corridors (NH-66, NH-544, NH-766, SH-1, NH-85), and 4 major river systems with 0 KB network transfer.
  • C
    6 Tactical Spectrum Modes: Cyber Obsidian, NVG Night Ops (green phosphor #00ff66 for night driving), Solar Daylight (high-lumen tropical contrast), Safety WCAG AAA, Satellite, and Terrain.
🗺️ Serverless PMTiles Range Engine

Resylix utilizes PMTiles binary archives. By issuing HTTP Range requests against local device storage, zoom levels 1 to 14 are retrieved in microseconds without needing a tile server (Mapbox, GeoServer, or TileServer GL).

⚡ Cold Start Tile Render: < 15 milliseconds
